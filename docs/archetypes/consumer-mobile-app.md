# Archetype: Consumer mobile app

> Easy-to-use apps for everyday, non-technical people. The user has low patience, divided attention, and no manual. If it isn't obvious, it's broken.

## Who it's for

The general public on their phone, often first-time and infrequent users. The opposite of the [ops/admin panel](ops-admin-panel.md) expert — optimize for the **first 30 seconds**, not the 30th day.

## Must do ✅

- **One primary action per screen**, obvious and thumb-reachable.
- **Guide, don't assume** — sensible defaults, gentle onboarding, empty states that teach.
- **Forgiving** — easy undo, no dead ends, clear ways back.
- **Plain language** — no jargon; label icons.
- **Instant feedback** and perceived speed (skeletons, optimistic UI).
- **Works on cheap phones & bad networks.**

## Should avoid ❌

- ❌ Dense, multi-action screens that overwhelm.
- ❌ Hidden gestures as the *only* way to do something important.
- ❌ Jargon, unlabeled icons, or tiny tap targets.
- ❌ Long forms with no progress or save.
- ❌ Punishing mistakes (hard deletes, no undo).

## Notes from experience

> *(seed — to expand)* The single biggest lever is reducing each screen to one obvious next step. When testers hesitate, it's almost always because the screen offered three equally-weighted choices.
